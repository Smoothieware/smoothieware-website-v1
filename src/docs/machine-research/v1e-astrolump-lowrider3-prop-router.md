# Astrolump's LowRider 3 for Prop and Set Work

**Machine identity:** `astrolump`'s individual LowRider 3 router CNC. The owner described the machine as operational in January 2024 and said it had already taken on paid prop/set work. The cited thread contains no machine photographs or wiring diagram, so this dossier does not infer physical details beyond the owner's text.

**Novelty check:** On 2026-09-23, the repository was searched for `astrolump`, `4x5 foot live area`, and the thread title. No matching individual-machine dossier was found.

## Owner-reported machine and use

The owner reports a 4 × 5 ft live area (approximately 1,219 × 1,524 mm) and a V1 Jackpot controller. They said the first two cuts covered the machine's cost because a current prop project otherwise would have required outsourcing CNC work. The owner makes sets and props, and described the machine as taking on much of the CNC work they had previously paid shops to do. This is an owner-reported business outcome, not a measured machine capability or accuracy test.

The owner described the build and setup as straightforward with the V1 instructions and noted having about one year of 3D-printing and laser-CNC experience at the time. They praised the machine's precision but supplied no tolerance, test artifact, or measurement data.

## Commissioning issue: Z-axis drop at shutdown

The owner reported that the spindle dropped when power was switched off and was concerned that it could damage expensive work if the bit stopped in the wrong position. They considered a different lead-screw option and, after another user suggested printed parking blocks, replied that they had downloaded them to print. The thread does not confirm that the blocks were printed or installed, or report a verified resolution.

Other participants discussed their own spindle mounts, drivers, and ways of keeping motors energized. Those statements refer to their machines and must not be attributed to astrolump's LR3 configuration.
