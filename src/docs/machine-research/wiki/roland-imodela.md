# Roland iModela

Research status: FabLab Region Nürnberg identifies a Roland iModela (iM-01), on permanent loan and restricted-use. Pinned wiki revision 3392 was rechecked 2026-09-28.

## Wiki-listed specification

The lab page describes a small-format hobby mill for soft materials, including modelling wax, acrylic and Styrofoam, and explicitly excludes conductive materials such as metal and carbon. PCB milling is a possible use but is limited by the small motor; the machine is not presented as a general metal mill. Maximum XYZ work area is 86 × 55 × 26 mm, maximum collet-tip-to-table distance 55 mm, and maximum workpiece mass 200 g. The XYZ motors are stepper motors. The wiki lists feed ranges of 6–240 mm/min on XY and 6–180 mm/min on Z. Software resolution is 0.01 mm/step for RML-1 and 0.001 mm/step for NC code; the listed mechanical microstep resolution is 0.000186 mm/step. Supported command sets are RML-1 and NC code. These are wiki values, not independently measured values.

## Wiki-recorded use and tools

The wiki identifies the machine as an iModela and documents PCB isolation milling. It records Eagle, pcp-gcode, and an iModela-specific Eagle pcb-gcode profile installed on the dedicated station. The article notes that the machine’s tool spindle accepts 2.34 mm tooling; because the lab’s other mill used 1/8-inch tools, a custom spindle adaptation was planned and its dimensions were copied locally on the wiki. The page does not establish whether that adapter was later made or installed.

The accessible machine page also contains software/file-format information for iModela Creator, Virtual MODELA, MODELA Player, 3D Engrave, SFEdit2 and ClickMILL. These are listed as available products/file types in the wiki, not as current vendor-supported versions.

## Connections and pinout

No motor, limit-switch, controller, spindle-power, or communication pinout appears in the inspected wiki page, pinned revision 3392. The 2.34 mm measurement describes the tool shank/spindle fit, not an electrical connector.
