# CNCArena/CNCzone chain-drive plasma-table build (Glenn / glasswoodmetal)

**Machine class:** an early DIY CNC plasma table described in a CNCzone project-log thread. The thread contains more than one participant's machine; this dossier isolates the initial builder's machine only where attribution is explicit.  
**Evidence state:** partial build and troubleshooting history; the builder later says this machine was dismantled and scrapped. Not an operational reference.  
**Novelty check:** exact thread/build identity not found in the current machine-control survey HTML or sibling assets on 2026-09-23.

## Forum-reported machine

The initial builder describes an approximately 3 ft square (914 mm) XY table, two 24 V wheelchair motors, and an intention to construct an inexpensive CNC plasma cutter for metal sculpture. The machine was initially crude and the author was still choosing control electronics and software. Later posts in the thread document recycled construction parts, skateboard bearings, threaded rod/lead screws, a web-purchased controller, and a test in which a motor was made to turn.

A later post from the same builder says the table was about 90% recycled and that the controller had been connected to a PC. The forum records subsequent mechanical revisions and electrical-noise problems with a vintage ESAB 875 plasma source: the author reports high-frequency arc interference affecting the PC/controller and motors, then describes a microwave-oven-case enclosure for the controller, ferrite cores, and shielded DB9 motor cables. The same post says the machine was eventually dismantled and scrapped. These are historical troubleshooting reports, not validated EMC or safety instructions.

The thread's title references a chain drive, and later replies mention replacing an X-axis belt with chain, but the evidence is a multi-author project log. Do not assume every described change belongs to the first machine without checking the post author and sequence.

## Operation / pinout

The inspected posts discuss a plasma-cutting objective, controller/software selection, and motion interference. They do not provide a verified connector pinout, complete axis layout, final control board identity, torch-height control configuration, limit/home map, or reproducible operating sequence. The machine's reported scrapped state makes this a historical case study, not a setup guide.
