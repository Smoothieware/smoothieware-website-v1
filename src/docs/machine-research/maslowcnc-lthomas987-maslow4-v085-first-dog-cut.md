# L. Thomas's Maslow 4 and First Dog-Profile Cuts

**Machine identity:** `lthomas987`'s individual Maslow router, built in March 2024 and brought back to calibration/cutting in November after a long pause. The post labels the topic with the `maslow4` tag but the owner calls it “my router”; this dossier treats it as the owner's Maslow 4 based on that forum context and records no unreported hardware details.

**Novelty check:** On 2026-09-23, the research directory was searched for `lthomas987`, the thread title, the attached photo names, and the reported v0.85/v0.72 calibration comparison. No matching individual-machine dossier was found.

## Calibration history and first cuts

The owner said the router arrived and was assembled in March 2024. During the early calibration attempts, they were among the users who experienced a belt being eaten during calibration. Repeated attempts continued through spring; the owner then paused the project over summer. In November, they tried firmware v0.85 after their previous attempt on v0.72 and reported that calibration went well. They then cut a dog silhouette from plywood scrap.

They repeated the cut on another scrap piece and said the router ran off the edge. The owner proposed a user-interface feature to jog to the outer corners of the design's bounding box before cutting. A Maslow team member replied that they had been doing this manually by jogging the machine and agreed a dedicated button would be faster. The thread does not report whether a bounding-box feature was implemented or whether the second cut's boundary issue was otherwise resolved.

## Owner-reported cutting setup

In response to a request for operating details, the owner reported:

- Material: 1/2-in. plywood of unknown origin, described as inexpensive B/C grade.
- Bit: 1/4-in. compression bit.
- Router: DeWalt 611, speed dial at 5; RPM was unknown.
- XY movement: approximately 500 mm/min, described as slow relative to other users.
- Cut depth: 0.2 in. per pass, as stated by the owner.

The owner said they were intentionally proceeding conservatively and thought they might be able to use more aggressive settings later. No measured dimensional accuracy, completed production job, or controlled feed/speed comparison is reported.
