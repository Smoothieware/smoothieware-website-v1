Complete wiki-researched machine dossier

Just Add Sharks Silvertail A0 laser cutter

Wiki evidence status: exact machine model independently named by London Hackspace and Bristol Hackspace. London records a 130 W tube and 1200 × 900 mm cutting area. The two wiki pages establish the same model family, but do not prove that the two sites have identical hardware revisions.

Identity and operation

London Hackspace identifies its machine as “Just Add Sharks Silvertail A0,” purchased by pledge in 2014. Its wiki lists a 1200 × 900 mm cutting area and a 130 W laser tube. It requires induction and membership, and it stresses continuous supervision because of fire risk. Its instructions require the CO2 extinguisher, water pump and extractor to be ready, and coolant at 22 ± 3 °C. The operating page describes LightBurn, places the software origin at the bed's top-left corner, and explains focusing relative to a ruler on the cutting bed.

Bristol Hackspace's current equipment catalogue separately lists an “A0 Laser Cutter - Just Add Sharks Silvertail,” corroborating the model name at a second wiki site. That listing does not expose a Bristol-specific power or work-area value.

Pinout and diagram evidence

The London Hackspace LightBurn repository supplies an MKS V1.0 Smoothieboard-clone config snapshot. It lists X STEP 2.2!, DIR 2.3!, ENABLE 0.4; Y STEP 0.19!, DIR 0.20!, ENABLE 0.10; Z STEP 2.13!, DIR 0.11!, ENABLE 0.19; X-min 1.29!^, X-max 1.28!^, Y-min 1.27!^, Y-max 1.26!^; Z-max 1.24!^ is defined despite gamma_limit_enable false and home_to_min, while Z-min 1.25^ is commented out; laser PWM is 2.0 (20 µs) and M3/M5 fire output is 1.23. These are logical configuration entries, not physical connector positions or proof of fitted switches. The config gives no physical switch-terminal layouts or separate endstop GND/return contacts, so those remain unknown rather than inferred. The source assigns Y STEP and Z ENABLE to the same MCU pin P0.19; this unresolved conflict is not silently corrected. The MYJG-80R manual reference names H, L, P, G, IN and 5V control terminals. The two dotted guesses are PWM.1 to IN through an unspecified conversion interface and PWM.3 GND to G as a possible signal reference. H and L stay OPEN: firmware semantics and terminal polarity do not reveal the installed carrier conversion or endpoint. DOTTED = GUESS. The historical MKS +5 V level-shift note does not qualify SmoothieBox voltage, polarity, isolation or wiring. No route is wiring guidance.

Supplemental controller and operating-state history

London Hackspace upgrade notes distinguish the stock LaserCut 5.3 setup from a later Smoothieware/LightBurn arrangement. They describe a right-rear home and left-front origin; the operation page gives a top-left software origin. These different source states do not establish one installed coordinate configuration.

The upgrade notes report an ACnode relay that de-powers the Smoothieboard, while the repository says it cuts +5 V. Relay terminals, ratings and safety-chain topology are undocumented. The config documents a RepRap Discount GLCD, but the wiki notes differ on panel state; neither confirms fitted wiring.

The machine page lists a stock Reci Z2 tube, MYJG-80R supply, Leetro MPC6515C v2.0 controller and LaserCut 5.3. This historical Leetro reference remains separate from the Smoothieware/LightBurn configuration. No DB25 is evidenced for either controller variant.
