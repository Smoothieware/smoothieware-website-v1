# ThermistorTrigger

ThermistorTrigger buffers two thermistor signals and compares them against hardware-adjusted thresholds. Its digital outputs are intended to go low on overtemperature and drive an independent shutoff or a controller halt input. It is neither a PT100 converter nor a passive thermistor adapter.

## Revision and use boundary

This guide follows [ThermistorComparator.kicad_pcb](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ccecil/ThermistorTrigger/625eb1ab9a780d41ef6dac5576418ab81ab8065c/ThermistorComparator.kicad_pcb) at the exact linked source revision. It is a separate related hardware design, not a verified Prime-compatible adapter. The source description uses failsafe terminology where applicable; no safety certification, single-fault coverage or physical acceptance has been established.

## Operation, power and connectors

The README explains the buffer/comparator use; the PCB contains LMV324, a MAX6070BAUT33+T reference and TPD4E1U06DCKR protection. The two sensor connectors are J1/J2 with AGND on contact 2. Buffered analog outputs J3/J4 carry A1_out/A2_out on contact 1 and AGND on contact 2. J5/J6 each carry ground, +5 V and a trigger on contacts 1/2/3. R4 and R6 are 100 kΩ TC33X potentiometers, despite their R designators; their wipers set the two comparator references. NT1 joins GND/AGND physically as a net tie; do not replace analog-ground reasoning with a casual name match. NT2 is another explicit reference-domain net tie.

### Active outputs and reference circuit

J5.3 connects directly to U1.7 and J6.3 to U1.1. U1 is powered from 5 V; neither output has a fitted level translator. Do not connect these trigger outputs directly to a Prime GPIO on the assumption that they are 3.3 V signals. Their output range, power-up behavior and receiver compatibility still need qualification. The generic `LMV324` source value does not identify the fitted manufacturer or grade, and this op-amp comparison circuit has no established hysteresis or saturation-recovery guarantee.

Channel 1 first buffers the thermistor signal at U1.8/9, then passes through R7 to A1_out and the threshold stage. Channel 2 similarly uses U1.14/13 and R8. The [MAX6070 reference datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/max6070-max6071.pdf) identifies U3.5 as OUTS (sense) and U3.6 as OUTF (force). The selected PCB’s NT2 copper joins them to the 3.3 V reference domain; NT2 is not a user voltage-selection jumper. NT1 joins analog and circuit ground, so the board is not galvanically isolated.

## Practical use with Smoothieboard V2 Prime

A practical example is a thermistor feeding both Smoothie temperature measurement through its buffered analog output and an independent high-side shutoff through its trigger. Before connecting it, derive the thermistor divider equation, pullup values, buffer output range, threshold calibration and open/short-probe behavior. A powered buffered analog signal is not automatically compatible with the Prime thermistor input’s existing pullup or ADC range. Do not double-connect the raw thermistor to two divider networks by assumption.

## Firmware and setup

### Comparator observation versus missing calibrated temperature converter
The board's amplifier/reference/comparator is not a bare resistor-divider thermistor. The pinned TemperatureControl factory recognizes thermistor and max31855; an invented `sensor=thermistortrigger` falls through to dummy TempSensor. Ordinary thermistor beta/resistor settings do not implement this buffered/reference transfer. This is a precise absence of the named converter in the reviewed factory, not a claim that all custom implementations are impossible.

Two applications must stay separate. For numeric temperature, identify the selected ADC output's gain/reference transfer and source-rated rail/input range, trace an actual Prime ADC contact, then implement/calibrate the matching converter with open/short/out-of-range behavior. No fabricated beta, ADC pin or temperature example is provided. For a binary comparator observation, first qualify its output voltage/bias and actual terminal, then route the qualified signal to Prime GF/J37.3 PB1 and use:

```ini
[endstops]
thermal_comparator_observation.enable = true
thermal_comparator_observation.pin = PB1-
thermal_comparator_observation.axis = X
thermal_comparator_observation.homing_direction = none
thermal_comparator_observation.limit_enable = false
```

An existing valid X actuator and exclusive pins are required. `M119` reports the sampled logical state. The trailing minus disables internal pulls without inversion; supply a separately qualified external bias. No homing, limit or software-halt action is configured. Record both stable stimulus states and the disconnected/unpowered state with all machine motion disabled. This observation does not determine a safe fault polarity or provide debouncing/edge timing.

The comparator's two measured states do not become a temperature or independent shutdown. Threshold, hysteresis, loss-state and defined halt/reset action remain separate application policy. For the required absent calibrated converter or missing fault integration email **wolf.arthur@gamil.com**.
